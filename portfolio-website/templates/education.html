Bachelor's and Master's Degrees at Politecnico di Torino.

Master's Degree in Mechatronic Engineering

Politecnico di Torino — Turin, Italy

Specialized in mechatronic engineering with a focus on automatic control, robotics, electronics, automation, embedded systems, and electric mobility technologies.

First Year

Digital Control Technologies and Architectures

Studied digital control systems and the design of discrete-time controllers for mechatronic applications, including modelling, simulation, and implementation using MATLAB and Simulink.

Modelling and Simulation of Mechatronic Systems

Developed mathematical models and simulations of dynamic mechatronic systems, with applications to mechanical, electrical, and automotive systems.

Electronic Systems for Mechatronics

Studied electronic systems used in mechatronic applications, including analog and digital electronics, signal acquisition, processing, and embedded electronic architectures.

Estimation, Filtering and System Identification

Studied techniques for identifying dynamic system models from experimental data, together with estimation and filtering methods for systems affected by noise and uncertainty.

Automatic Control

The course will address the fundamentals of dynamical systems analysis and of the design of simple feedback controllers.

Model-Based Software Design

Studied model-based approaches for embedded software development, including system modelling, simulation, validation, and automotive functional safety concepts.

Robotics

Studied robotic systems with emphasis on robot kinematics, dynamics, trajectory generation, and control of industrial and mobile robots.

Technologies for eMobility

Specialized coursework focused on electric mobility, electric powertrains, electrical machines, power electronics, vehicle control, and connected vehicle technologies.

Electrical Technologies for eMobility

Studied the main components of electric traction systems, including batteries, power electronic converters, and electrical machines, with emphasis on their integration into electric powertrains.

Automotive Control Systems

Studied modelling and control of automotive systems, including vehicle dynamics, chassis systems, engines, and drivetrains. Applied control techniques through simulation and performance analysis.

Electric Motor Drives for ePowertrains

Studied electric motor drives for electric and hybrid vehicles, including AC motor modelling, power electronics, torque control, motor identification, efficiency optimization, and digital control algorithms.

Networking Technologies for Connected Vehicles

Studied communication and networking technologies used in connected vehicles, including vehicle networks, vehicle-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-infrastructure communication, as well as vehicle localization systems.

Bachelor's Degree in Mechanical Engineering

Politecnico di Torino — Turin, Italy

The degree provided a broad foundation in mechanical engineering, including mechanics, materials, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, machine design, manufacturing, electrical engineering, and industrial systems.

First Year

Chemistry

Provides the fundamental chemistry needed by an engineer, covering atomic structure, periodicity, chemical bonding, stoichiometry, gases and solutions, chemical equilibrium, thermodynamics, electrochemistry, and basic organic chemistry.

Computer Sciences

Introduces students to the cultural and technological foundations of computer science and teaches programming as a tool for solving realistic engineering problems.

Mathematical Analysis I

Establishes the mathematical foundations needed for engineering, including logical reasoning, differential and integral calculus for functions of one variable, and applications to ordinary differential equations.

Engineering Drawing

Teaches the formal representation of mechanical components through technical drawings, including shape, dimensions, manufacturing information, dimensional and geometrical tolerances.

Linear Algebra and Geometry

Covers the mathematical tools of linear algebra and geometry required in engineering, including vector spaces, matrices, linear systems, transformations, and geometric concepts.

Physics I

Provides the fundamental scientific basis for engineering physics, with emphasis on mechanics, thermodynamics, and fundamental physical laws.

Second Year

Fundamentals of Strength of Materials

Introduces the mechanical behaviour and strength of materials and structural elements, including stresses, strains, axial loading, bending, torsion, shear, buckling, and fatigue.

Mathematical Analysis II

Extends mathematical analysis to more advanced engineering applications, including sequences and series, Fourier series, Laplace transforms, functions of several variables, and multiple integrals.

Physics II

Develops further physical principles relevant to engineering, building on Physics I and introducing topics such as electromagnetism and other areas of modern physics.

Science and Technology of Materials

Examines the relationship between material structure, properties, processing, and performance. Covers metals, polymers, ceramics, glasses, composites, defects, deformation, phase diagrams, strengthening, and corrosion.

Applied Mechanics

Applies mechanics to real mechanical systems and power transmission. Topics include rigid-body mechanics, gears, belt drives, shafts, joints, screws, clutches, brakes, bearings, dynamics, and vibrations.

Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics and Heat Transfer

Develops the ability to analyse engineering systems using thermodynamics and heat-transfer principles, including the first and second laws of thermodynamics, energy transfers, efficiency, and heat transfer.

Methodologies for Inspection and Quality Control

Introduces engineering methods used to inspect manufactured components and evaluate their quality, providing an overview of inspection and quality-control techniques used in industry.

Third Year

Fluid Mechanics

Introduces the principles required to analyse fluids at rest and in motion, including fluid properties, pressure, conservation laws, and fluid flow in engineering systems.

Fundamentals of Machine Design

Introduces the principles used to design mechanical components and machines, connecting loads, stresses, material properties, component geometry, and safety requirements to mechanical design.

Introduction to Electrical Engineering / Electrical Machines

Provides the fundamentals of electrical engineering, including DC and AC circuits, electrical components, circuit analysis, electrical power systems, and the principles and applications of electrical machines.

Fundamentals of Fluid Machinery

Covers thermal and hydraulic machines and the fundamentals of fluid-power systems, including operating principles, thermodynamic and fluid-dynamic behaviour, performance, off-design operation, and hydraulic and pneumatic systems.

Industrial Plants and Safety

Introduces the design and management of industrial plants, including factory layout, plant configuration, and cost management. Also covers workplace safety, regulations, and industrial risk management.

Manufacturing Processes

Covers the principal manufacturing processes used to transform materials into engineering components, including manufacturing technologies, process selection, process capabilities, product design, and quality.
