Master's Thesis

  • Project Goal: Developed data-driven virtual temperature sensors for UAV electric propulsion systems to replace expensive, fragile Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) optical sensors.
  • Methodology: Designed and evaluated deep learning sequence models—including LSTMs, GRUs, minLSTMs/minGRUs, xLSTMs, and CNNs—to estimate internal motor temperatures using non-intrusive electrical parameters (current, voltage, speed, and resistance).
  • Implementation: Built an experimental test bench using an Arduino Due, ACS712 current sensors, and FBG interrogators to collect real-time data and train multi-layer neural network architectures.
  • Key Finding: Successfully demonstrated that neural networks can accurately infer thermal dynamics from electrical signals, offering a cost-effective alternative for UAV health monitoring.

Bachelor's Thesis

The purpose of this project is to elaborate on the method of choosing a suitable material for a light pressure vessel which has a specific radius, must not fail by yielding, must not fail by fast fracture and must also be economically feasible while minimizing the mass. This study creates a “min-max” problem. While trying to find the minimum mass there are only two free variables to choose from: Wall thickness t of the vessel and the choice of material. This is especially useful in engineering fields such as in the petroleum oil and gas industry, pharmaceuticals and food and beverage industries.

Fundamentals of Machine Design Final Project

The project performs a structural and mechanical verification analysis of a gearbox assembly, focusing on the shaft, bearings, and gears:

  • Shaft Analysis & Verification: Evaluates internal forces, bending moments, cross-sectional areas, section moduli, and stresses (normal, bending, torsional, and equivalent) along the shaft.
  • Bearing Verification: Focuses on tapered roller bearings (designation 32922 in a face-to-face arrangement). It covers static load capacity, safety factors, lubricant viscosity requirement/selection, and SKF rating life analysis.
  • Gear Verification: Evaluates gear bending and contact stress (pitting resistance) using AGMA standard methodology.

Manufacturing Processes Final Project

The aim of this project was the development and study of the technological process for the manufacturing of a forged part, which is later to be machined to the required dimensions and tolerances. The core technological process used is the closed die forging. The forging will have to be analyzed and simulated in the QForm 3D software. The aim of the simulations is to determine the adequate technological processes, as well as the many parameters, to achieve the desired part without any defects and keeping in mind the economical aspect of the manufacturing. The manufacturing process design will have to be developed according to the guidelines from the EN DIN 10243, EN10277-2 and the DIN 7523 standard for Steel die forgings. Electrical Technologies for eMobility Final Project

This project focuses on designing and modeling a high-efficiency electric vehicle (xEV) powertrain architecture consisting of a traction high-voltage battery, a bidirectional DC-DC boost converter, a three-phase inverter, and a Surface-mounted Permanent Magnet (SPM) synchronous motor.

Key Components and Methodology

  • Bidirectional DC-DC Boost Converter: Sized to manage power transfer between the HV battery and the DC link. Includes a cascaded control structure (inner current loop operating at 2.5 kHz bandwidth and outer voltage loop at 2.0 kHz) to regulate the output voltage (Vdc = 400 V) under varying current demands.
  • Motor Analysis & Identification: Evaluated using flux maps and MTPA/MTPV curves to classify the drive machine as an SPM synchronous motor (Rs = 0.1773 Ω, p = 2).
  • Inverter Sizing: Engineered to meet maximum torque (133.9 Nm) and speed requirements (6837.8 rpm). Derived key specifications include a maximum current rating of 237.71 A, a DC-link supply voltage of 327.84 V (utilizing zero-sequence injection), and a switching frequency (fsw) of 4559 Hz.
  • Field-Oriented Control (FOC): Implemented via Current Vector Control (CVC-FOC) using decoupled d-q axis PI controllers with anti-windup voltage saturation and feedforward decoupling to optimize torque generation and dynamic response.
